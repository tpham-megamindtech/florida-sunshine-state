The story of Florida's next economy is being written in places like Naples. In the nation's brightest state, clean-energy entrepreneurs argue the real boom is only starting.

That is the backdrop for a change that has been building quietly and is now impossible to ignore. Across Naples and out toward Tampa, the same patterns keep surfacing, and the people closest to them have stopped hedging about where things are headed.

"The interest is real, but the work is only getting started." says Lena Duarte, a managing partner who has watched it unfold up close. The details vary from one case to the next, but the throughline is hard to miss.

## Why it matters now

The stakes are bigger than one company or one quarter. A few things stand out to the people watching Naples most closely:

- Lower costs that let young firms run longer on less
- Capital that used to skip Florida is now staying put
- Infrastructure finally catching up with the growth
- A widening talent pool as workers relocate to the state

None of it happens by accident. It takes money, timing, and a tolerance for the false starts that come with doing something before it is obvious. What stands out is how many of those ingredients now sit within reach in Naples.

## The bigger picture

Zoom out and this looks less like a single story than a place finding another gear. Julian Osei, a venture investor in Tampa, is quick to note that plenty could still go sideways. "People underestimate how much has changed here in a short time." But the ambition is real, and so is the momentum behind it.

The work is far from finished, but the direction is hard to mistake.
