The story of Florida's next economy is being written in places like Jacksonville. As money changes hands between generations, the advisers of Southwest Florida are rewriting the playbook.

That is the backdrop for a change that has been building quietly and is now impossible to ignore. Across Jacksonville and out toward Tampa, the same patterns keep surfacing, and the people closest to them have stopped hedging about where things are headed.

"The interest is real, but the work is only getting started." says Nadia Ellison, a founder who has watched it unfold up close. The details vary from one case to the next, but the throughline is hard to miss.

## Why it matters now

The stakes are bigger than one company or one quarter. A few things stand out to the people watching Jacksonville most closely:

- Infrastructure finally catching up with the growth
- Lower costs that let young firms run longer on less
- A tighter network of founders sharing hard lessons
- Local customers willing to try something new

None of it happens by accident. It takes money, timing, and a tolerance for the false starts that come with doing something before it is obvious. What stands out is how many of those ingredients now sit within reach in Jacksonville.

## The bigger picture

Zoom out and this looks less like a single story than a place finding another gear. Celeste Reyes, a managing partner in Tampa, is quick to note that plenty could still go sideways. "The pieces have been coming together for a while — most people just weren't looking in the right place." But the ambition is real, and so is the momentum behind it.

For now, the momentum in Jacksonville looks less like a moment than a trend.
