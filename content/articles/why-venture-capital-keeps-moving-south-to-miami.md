Florida's economy has always run on more than sunshine, and Naples is proof. Fund managers who once flew in for the winter are now signing office leases and staying for the deals.

That is the backdrop for a change that has been building quietly and is now impossible to ignore. Across Naples and out toward West Palm Beach, the same patterns keep surfacing, and the people closest to them have stopped hedging about where things are headed.

"People underestimate how much has changed here in a short time." says Ingrid Pereira, a founder who has watched it unfold up close. The details vary from one case to the next, but the throughline is hard to miss.

## Why it matters now

The stakes are bigger than one company or one quarter. A few things stand out to the people watching Naples most closely:

- Capital that used to skip Florida is now staying put
- Infrastructure finally catching up with the growth
- A widening talent pool as workers relocate to the state
- Local customers willing to try something new

None of it happens by accident. It takes money, timing, and a tolerance for the false starts that come with doing something before it is obvious. What stands out is how many of those ingredients now sit within reach in Naples.

## The bigger picture

Zoom out and this looks less like a single story than a place finding another gear. Ingrid Hawkins, an economic-development director in West Palm Beach, is quick to note that plenty could still go sideways. "The pieces have been coming together for a while — most people just weren't looking in the right place." But the ambition is real, and so is the momentum behind it.

Whatever comes next, Naples has earned a place in the conversation.
